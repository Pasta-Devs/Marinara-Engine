# Game Mode: MRPG Classes

Every new game plays on **MRPG** (Marinara RPG) classes, including games made from an Experience. Each party member has a class, gains levels from the fights the party wins, and opens new classes as they grow. This page is the player's guide to them: choosing a class, what the stats mean, how levels and promotions work, and the character sheet.

Two kinds of game do not use MRPG classes:

- **Legacy games.** Games made before MRPG classes existed keep their **Legacy** sheets until you choose to move them over. See [Move a Legacy game to MRPG](#move-a-legacy-game-to-mrpg).
- **Ruleset games.** A game played with a ruleset, such as 5e (SRD 5.1), uses the ruleset's own sheets. See [Playing with a Ruleset](rulesets.md).

For how a fight plays on MRPG classes, see [Battles in an MRPG game](combat.md#battles-in-an-mrpg-game).

## How it works

- Each character trains in two **trees** out of eight: a **primary tree** you choose, and a **sister tree** their temperament opens.
- They start the game in the base class of their primary tree, at level 1.
- Winning fights gives experience. A character's stats come from their **active class** and rise as that class gains levels.
- At class level 10 a class can be promoted to an advanced class, and at class level 20 to a master class. Levels stop at 30.
- Outside battle, a character can switch to any class they have opened, and carry up to four skills from other classes they trained in.

## The eight trees

| Tree | Row | How it fights | Sister tree for these temperaments |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Blade** | Front | Weapon fighters who meet the enemy head-on. | Reckless |
| **Guard** | Front | Shield-bearers who take the blows meant for their allies and make the enemy come to them. | Protective, Honourable |
| **Scout** | Front | Fast opportunists who strike, dodge and steal. | Opportunistic, Cowardly |
| **Marksman** | Back | Archers who switch ammunition to suit the target. | Patient, Cautious |
| **Channeler** | Back | Mages of fire, ice, lightning, wind, wood, earth, water and metal. | Arrogant, Methodical |
| **Hexer** | Back | Curse-casters who weaken foes and drain their HP. | Cruel |
| **Mender** | Back | Healers who keep the party standing and cure what ails it. | Supportive |
| **Herald** | Back | Battle leaders who give orders and sing songs that make the party stronger. | Coordinated, Disciplined |

A party with a mix of trees does best. Melee hits on the back row deal half damage, so the back-row trees rely on the front row to hold the line (see [Rows](combat.md#rows)).

### Classes in each tree

Each tree has seven classes. The base class promotes at class level 10 to one of two advanced classes, and each of those promotes at class level 20 to its master class. A third line, in the last column, is open only to a **Pure** character: one whose temperament points back at their own primary tree, so they have no sister tree and train in that one tree alone.

| Tree | Base class | Advanced (10) → Master (20) | Pure only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blade | Blade | Duelist → Headsman; Bloodsworn → Cleaver | Weaponmaster → Paragon of Arms |
| Guard | Guard | Sentinel → Stonewall; Warden → Elder Warden | Aegis → Bulwark |
| Scout | Scout | Rogue → Phantom; Skirmisher → Harrier | Shade → Shroud |
| Marksman | Marksman | Sharpshooter → Farshot; Tracker → Huntsmaster | Artillerist → Siegebreaker |
| Channeler | Channeler | Elementalist → Stormcaller; Rune Scholar → Runelord | Astral Adept → Starweaver |
| Hexer | Hexer | Witch → Carrion Lord; Dreamcaller → Dreamweaver | Nullifier → Void Sovereign |
| Mender | Mender | Cleric → Saint; Apothecary → Panacean | Lifebinder → Lightbearer |
| Herald | Herald | Cantor → Maestro; Field Captain → Marshal | Standard-Bearer → Lodestar |

The **Classes** tab of the character sheet describes every class and lists its skills with the class level each is learned at.

## Choose a character's trees

**Primary tree.** Pick it before the game, in the **Game Mode** section of a library character's or persona's **Stats** tab (see [MRPG picks on a card](../characters/colors-and-stats.md#mrpg-picks-on-a-card)). Leave it on **Auto** and it is read from the character's description, and in the game from their class and abilities too. A character whose card names no tree starts as a Blade. Until a character gains their first experience, you can also change it in the game, on the **Classes** tab of their sheet under **Main tree**.

**Strong and weak stats.** The same card section lets you pick up to two stats the character is **Strong in** and one they are **Weak in**. Every game rolls each character's hidden growth afresh, and these picks lean that roll. They set no numbers.

**Sister tree.** When the game makes a character's game card, the Game Master reads their temperament from their Description and personality: how they go about a fight. That temperament opens the sister tree in the table above. Write the Description with it in mind if you want a particular sister tree. The sheet shows the temperament beside the sister tree, for example "from Cautious". The story can change a character's temperament later, but their sister tree stays the one they started with.

A character whose temperament opens their own primary tree, such as a Reckless Blade, is **Pure**: they train in one tree only, and its pure-only line opens to them.

## What the stats mean

| Stat | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **HP** | Health. A character who reaches 0 falls. A fight's HP bar is larger than the stat and grows with level. |
| **Strength** (Str) | Damage with weapons. Also raises Stamina. |
| **Magic** (Mag) | Damage of spells and of staffs, wands and foci, and the power of healing. Also sets MP. |
| **Skill** (Skl) | Accuracy and critical hits. |
| **Speed** (Spd) | How often the character acts, and how well they dodge. Also raises Stamina. |
| **Defense** (Def) | Cuts damage from weapons and other physical attacks. |
| **Resistance** (Res) | Cuts damage from magic. |
| **Luck** (Lck) | A little accuracy and dodging, and protection from critical hits. |

Two resources pay for skills:

- **MP** pays for magic. It is 8 plus twice Magic, and it does not come back during a fight.
- **Stamina** (ST) pays for everything else. It is 10 plus Strength plus half of Speed, and some comes back every turn, more on a turn spent defending.

The same stats are used for skill checks outside battle. See [Skill checks](#skill-checks).

## Levels and experience

**Experience.** A won fight pays experience for every foe the party beat, including foes that surrendered or fled. Half of it is shared evenly by everyone who fought, and half by how much each did: damage, healing and support. Foes below a character's level pay less and foes above it pay more, and a character below the party's average level earns half as much again. A lost or fled fight pays nothing.

**Character level.** Every 100 experience is a level, up to 30. The battle screen's end panel, **What the party gained**, shows the experience, each level gained and the stats that rose.

**Class level.** The active class earns the same experience toward its own class level. A class trained below half the character's level earns double, so a class taken up late catches up. A character's stats come from their active class at its class level: each class level rolls a gain for every stat, by the class's growth and the character's hidden aptitudes, and at least two stats rise each time.

This means a newly taken up class starts with a level 1 body. Switch to a fresh class between fights you can afford, and switch back when you need the strength you built.

**Promotion.** When a class reaches class level 10, its advanced classes open; at class level 20, its master class opens. The end panel says **A promotion is open**. Promote from the **Classes** tab with **Promote to** and the class name. The promoted class starts at its parent's class level, adds a one-time stat bonus, and fills any free carry slots with the actives of the classes it came from.

Growth rates are hidden. With **Debug** on, the **Status** tab shows each stat's exact chance to rise.

## Switch classes and carry skills

Classes change outside battle, on the **Classes** tab. Pick a class to see its skills and what switching would leave behind, then choose **Take up** (a class not yet trained) or **Switch to** (one trained before, at the class level it reached).

A character uses only their active class's skills, plus up to four skills **carried** from other classes they trained in. Choose them on the **Skills** tab under **Carry from other classes**. A carried skill runs on the character's own stats. Some skills belong to a whole tree, such as a Guard's Cover, and every class of that tree keeps them without a slot. When switching would drop a skill you have been using, the sheet warns you first so you can carry it.

## The character sheet

Open a party member's sheet from **Character Profiles**. It has four tabs:

- **Status**: level, experience, the active class, the stats with gear counted, and HP, MP and Stamina as a fight starts. **Row** sets whether they stand in the front or back row in Turn-Based battles; each class has a default.
- **Classes**: the two trees and every class in them, with what is open, what each class needs, and the promotions.
- **Skills**: the active class's skills and the carried ones, with their costs.
- **Gear**: a **Weapon**, **Armour** and two **Accessory** slots, filled from what the party carries and the class can use. Each piece shows its stats before you equip it. An empty weapon or armour slot fights with the class's standard piece for their level.

The weapon decides what an attack runs on: a staff, wand or focus strikes with Magic against Resistance, and any other weapon with Strength against Defense.

**Tamed companions.** A Tracker can tame a weakened wild beast in a fight. It joins their companions, shown on the **Status** tab, up to eight. The active companion raises the stat it is strongest in while a Tracker or Huntsmaster class is active, more for higher-level and elite beasts. When the stable is full, release one to keep the newcomer. Companions are swapped outside battle.

**Permadeath.** With **Permadeath** on (a setup option on the wizard's **World** step, off by default), a party member who falls in a fight and is not saved before it ends is gone for good. With it off, the fallen get back up after the fight.

## Skill checks

Checks outside battle use the character's MRPG stats, the ones their active class gives them. Each skill leans on one stat, such as Stealth on Speed or Persuasion on Luck. See [Dice and Skill Checks](dice-and-skill-checks.md#how-the-result-is-decided) for the full list and the bonus each stat gives.

## New party members and shared games

**Someone who joins a game under way.** A companion who joins after the game began comes in at the level the Game Master gives them. Most start low, and a seasoned fighter may start near the party's level. The Game Master can also give them faster growth in some stats, for a raw talent who will catch up, or better stats from the start, for someone already capable. A newcomer the Game Master says nothing about starts at level 1.

**Guests in a shared game.** Each guest builds their own character when they join the room: a name, a description, a **character concept** for the Game Master, and the same **Game Mode** picks a card has (starting tree, strong stats and weak stat). Choosing one of your personas fills these in from it. Use the concept to tell the Game Master who your character is and how you would like them to come into the story. The Game Master fits the character into the story and picks their sister tree.

- **A new game.** Everyone, host and guests, starts at level 1.
- **A game under way.** The Game Master also sets the guest's starting level, growth and stats, as for any newcomer.
- **A returning guest.** A guest's character stays in the game when they leave, with its level, gear and tamed beasts. While their player is away, the character sits out fights, earns no experience and is left out of scenes. When the guest comes back, they play the same character at the level they left it.

Each player's skill checks read their own character's stats.

## Move a Legacy game to MRPG

A Legacy game keeps its Legacy sheets for as long as you want. To move it over, open the **Journal**, and on the **All** tab find the **MRPG classes** card. Select **Upgrade**.

For each party member, the card shows:

- **Class.** The tree they start in, filled in from their library card's pick, or else from their Legacy class. You can change it.
- **Sister tree.** The tree their temperament opens. A character with no temperament yet gets one read from their Description when you upgrade.
- **Gear.** Each Legacy weapon and piece of armour they carry, and the MRPG piece it would become. Clear the box to leave one as it is.

When you confirm with **Upgrade the party**:

- Every party member starts at level 10, the top of their starting class, with each level's stats rolled as a level-up would. Their first promotion is open at once.
- Their card's class becomes their MRPG class. Their Legacy class and abilities move into a **Before the upgrade** note on the card, so the story still knows them. Their Legacy stats also stay on the card, but checks and fights no longer use them.
- Each accepted weapon or piece of armour becomes its MRPG piece and keeps its name, and they wear it if their class can.

> **Warning:** The upgrade is one-way. A game that moved to MRPG classes can't go back to Legacy sheets.

The upgrade is not offered during a fight. Finish the fight first. A game played with a ruleset can't be upgraded: it keeps its ruleset's sheets.
